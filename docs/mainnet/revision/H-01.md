# H-01 mitigation and executable interface limit

**High — unresolved, release blocking.** The independent severity in `../INDEPENDENT_REVIEW.md` is preserved. This author has not independently reassessed or accepted it. The originally independent segregated-funds proof remains unchanged in `test/mainnet/SharedExecutorReview.t.sol`. New mainnet regressions are in `QuotaMitigation.t.sol`; their actual-mainnet counterpart is `MainnetQuotaMitigationRehearsal`.

## What the router proves

The exact mainnet Universal Router exposes `msgSender()`, its current execute lock owner. The hook checks both its address and runtime. The manager supplies the key, direction, specified amount and executed pool deltas. These facts authenticate an **account's** E/S ledger, not the people or accounts using a shared executor. The router does not expose future TAKE recipients, final net settlement recipients, Permit2 funding owners or prefunding provenance through that hook interface. The external hook call's `msg.data` is its callback data, not the router's execute calldata. Empty hookData adds no identity evidence.

A wallet calling the router directly keeps its own E/S. A legitimate smart account can do the same. Buying through a public shared executor credits that executor, including when the UNLK is delivered straight to the buyer. A later unrelated seller supplies only its own tokens and can spend the mature executor quota. This is not token theft from the buyer. It is loss of the buyer-associated shared sale capacity. The tests assert Alice's tokens do not move, the executor has no residual token custody, Bob cannot sell directly, and Bob receives his own sale proceeds through the executor.

The inherited recorded-router counterexamples also preserve identical callback observations for TAKE_ALL versus TAKE to another recipient, and user Permit2 funding versus router prefunding. Mainnet tests cover directed recipients, Permit2, multihop, partial cycles, alternate executors and ordinary transfers. A generic Quoter result is neither wallet authorization nor proof of a funding account. The pinned Quoter cannot settle because it necessarily reverts its unlock callback.

## Implemented reductions

1. **One-block maturity for all official buy credit.** E still records every executed buy immediately and never decreases. `spendableEligible(a)` subtracts only buy credit created in the current block. Before full unlock, `Q=max(0,floor(spendableEligible*p/10000)-S)`. This additional timing condition is explicit, not a change in E's meaning. Claims and the fixed 3% allocation are not delayed. Transfers do not create, move or mature anyone's credit. There is no owner exemption. Final unlock still permanently bypasses all quota and maturity checks; fixed fees continue.
2. **Optional immutable WalletExecutor.** Its immutable wallet alone can call `trade` or recover accidental tokens. It pulls input from the authenticated `msg.sender`, constructs the standard router plan internally, fixes output/refunds to that same wallet, bounds spending, clears both approvals, and guards reentrancy. There is no supplied sender, recipient, generic call target, delegatecall, backend signature or mutable allowlist. Its E/S belongs to the executor address and remains **separate** from the wallet's direct E/S. It cannot consume or merge direct-wallet rights. Smart-account wallets work. This is an additional integration, not a launch that requires a custom router.

The previous rejected attempt's arbitrary-from token pull is absent: `WalletExecutor.trade` authenticates `msg.sender == wallet` and calls `safeTransferFrom(msg.sender, ...)`. The failure test grants the executor a victim approval, calls it as another account and checks that the victim funds are untouched.

The optional executor prevents another caller from consuming that executor's quota. It cannot prove that the controlling wallet itself is not a multi-user account, sold controller, or delegated account. There is no claim of personhood or global isolation of pooled users.

## Capacity and economic limits

For an eligible buy of B UNLK at p=20%, the previous implementation immediately exposed floor(B/5) additional capacity. The mitigation exposes **zero** new capacity during the buying block, then **exactly the same floor(B/5)** starting the next block. An attacker with existing mature shared capacity needs zero new quota-generating IMD purchases: they need up to Q of their own UNLK, gas and the normal sale fees. They may use borrowed inventory or obtain it by an ordinary transfer. No victim funds, victim approval or stolen signature are required. Thus there is **no increased capital lower bound against mature H-01 pooling**. Its High severity is retained.

A fresh pooler who creates their own capacity for an extra sale X needs at least ceil(10000*(S+X)/p)-E mature eligible UNLK acquisition, ignoring integer rounding at the boundary. Initially the gross acquisition ratio is approximately five UNLK bought for each UNLK of fresh sale capacity. Those purchases pay the unchanged fee-inclusive IMD cost; holding across a block adds inventory exposure/financing and defeats an atomic flash loan based solely on newly minted credit. There is no guaranteed multi-block financing cost. Before and after maturity, capacity remains address-scoped; it is never a transferable token or a token-transfer credit.

Exact-input buy of gross G IMD splits into B_IMD + treasuryFee = G, with treasuryFee within one raw unit of floor(75*B_IMD/10000). The pool's 1.25% input fee applies within B_IMD. Selling X UNLK pays the pool input fee in UNLK and 75 bps of the executed IMD output to the vault. The resulting effective cost is not exactly 2%. In the deterministic mainnet-runtime regression, Alice spends 100 IMD and receives 37,520,205.924135432073673623 UNLK. The exposed cap is 7,504,041.184827086414734724 UNLK before mitigation, zero for the buying block after mitigation, and the full 7,504,041.184827086414734724 again after maturity. Alice pays 0.744416873449131513 IMD treasury fee. Bob supplies exactly that cap in his own UNLK, consumes all of it and receives 19.901531076301633668 IMD; total treasury accrual becomes 0.894806277048388190 IMD. These figures include normal pool/treasury settlement, exclude gas and any price paid to obtain Bob’s inventory, and leave Alice’s token holdings unchanged. The same sequence is repeated on the recorded-mainnet fork. Market-dependent output amounts are not claimed as price guarantees.

| Attempt | Result after mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Same transaction buy and sell up to the new 20% cap | Reverts atomically, including E/S, fees and pool movement |
| Separate execute calls in the same block | Still no new buy capacity; cannot bypass with another UI |
| Split buys, commands, directed TAKE, hookData, router prefunding | All new credit stays with the authenticated initiator and immature through that block |
| Multihop auxiliary→IMD→UNLK | Standard routing works; UNLK credit follows initiator and has the same maturity |
| Cycle using already mature E | Allowed within that mature cap; consumed S cannot be recycled without new buys |
| Switch to another executor | Cannot borrow the first executor's or direct wallet's E; attacker can still find another executor with its own pooled E |
| Wait one block and sell someone else's mature shared capacity | Still works: explicit residual High proof |
| Transfer UNLK / native distributor claim | Transfer unrestricted and credits none; native claim independent, proven claim credit unchanged |
| WalletExecutor called by unrelated user | Rejected before funds move, even with existing wallet approval |
| Shared/delegated controlling smart account | Cannot infer separate underlying users; residual pooling remains |
| Unrelated pool | Outside the official hook's quota scope, as before |

Honest impact: a newly bought amount cannot support a sell in the same block; existing mature quota is usable and arbitrary ordinary transfers work. A wallet-scoped executor requires an extra call and approvals and keeps a separate ledger, which the preview explains. No fixed fee, milestone, principal protection, permanent final bypass or live launch rule changes.

## Incompatible stronger requirements

Global beneficial-user quota isolation, unrestricted smart accounts/shared execution, unrestricted token transfers and an unmodified public router with empty hookData cannot all be enforced by this callback. Two executions can have identical hook observations but different recipients/funders; any deterministic hook decision is identical. An immutable rejection of all contracts breaks legitimate accounts and is defeated as an identity test by delegation. Runtime allowlists do not solve identity and add forbidden policy. Arbitrary signatures identify a signing account but not the actual settlement parties unless the router exposes and enforces their binding. Recipient/funder settlement guarantees can be enforced inside the optional executor because it constructs the plan; they are not retroactively imposed on every public router call. Longer delays, quotas per transaction, or UI labels cannot distinguish a deliberate shared caller from an honest smart account. No stronger global prevention is claimed.
